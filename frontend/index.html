        Vishwas

        Forecast Trust Console

        Vishwas estimates how likely an already-issued NCMRWF and IMD medium-range forecast is to bust, and how much confidence to place in it, for each of 33 meteorological subdivisions and each lead day from Day 1 to Day 10, before the outcome is known. It does not forecast the weather.

                        Methodology

                        What it does

                        Does

                        • Takes an NCMRWF rainfall forecast that has already been issued, for Day 1 to Day 10.
                        • Estimates, for each of 33 meteorological subdivisions and each lead day, the probability that it will bust, before the outcome is known.
                        • Explains the estimate, shows the closest past case and says how much precedent the model has.

                        Does not

                        • Forecast the weather itself, or replace IMD or NCMRWF guidance.
                        • Issue public warnings.
                        • Replace the duty forecaster's judgement. It is a second opinion on how much to trust the first one.

                        What counts as a bust

                        A forecast busts when either condition holds (the two are checked separately and the trigger is recorded):

                        • Magnitude: the error is at least 25 mm, or at least the subdivision's 95th-percentile error for that lead day, whichever is larger. The percentile is fitted on training years only.
                        • Category: the rain / no-rain call was wrong, using IMD's rainy-day line of 2.5 mm.

                        Data

                        • Forecasts: NCMRWF's S2S reforecast archive (Unified Model coupled system, about 60 km), 1993 to 2015, one run per month, Days 1 to 10: 91,080 forecast rows.
                        • Observations: IMD gridded daily rainfall (0.25 degree), averaged over each subdivision using IMD's subdivision boundaries.
                        • Weather state at issue time: the forecast's own sea-level pressure, surface pressure and 10 m winds, plus the IMD rain observed the day before issue.
                        • In progress: NCMRWF's operational ensemble (NEPS, about 12 km, daily runs, 2017 to 2025) from the TIGGE archive, which will replace the 2015 limit.

                        Two kinds of confidence

                        Forecast confidence

                        100% minus the bust probability: how far the issued forecast can be trusted for this subdivision and lead day. It uses the warning colours.

                        Model self-confidence

                        How much precedent the model has for the case: enough past busts for this subdivision and lead day, a familiar pattern, inputs inside the range it was trained on, and agreement with similar past cases. It uses a separate violet scale, never the warning colours.

                        Big-miss watch

                        Most busts are rain / no-rain flips. A separate model estimates the risk that the forecast misses by a large amount (a magnitude bust) and flags it when that risk crosses a threshold set on held-out data. It is shown on the dashboard next to the bust probability and never changes it.

                        How well it works

                        Evaluated on 2013 to 2015, years the model never saw in training (11,880 forecasts). About 1 in 6 forecasts busts in these years.

                        0.541Bust-detection score (PR-AUC) of Vishwas
                        0.452Same score using the forecast amount alone
                        0.272Same score using past bust rates alone
                        0.02Calibration error: a stated 40% is close to 40%

                        The big-miss watch raises the share of large misses caught at Alert or above from 41% to 75%, for 2.5% more flagged forecasts.

                        Known limitations

                        • Days 1 to 3: little gain over the raw forecast amount. The archive has one run a month, so the signal from how a forecast changes between runs is missing.
                        • Large misses: the bust probability is driven mostly by rain / no-rain flips; the big-miss watch helps, but extreme events can still be missed (for example Tamil Nadu on 2 December 2015).
                        • Coastal and hilly subdivisions (Coastal Karnataka, Kerala, Konkan and Goa) are the weakest: the 60 km model resolves their terrain-driven rain poorly.
                        • Archive: the reforecast archive ends in 2015, so the console replays past cycles rather than showing today's forecast.
                        • Weather-event tags are rule-based labels from the forecast's pressure, winds and season, not a trained model.

                        How the sources combine into a bust label

                        For every past forecast, the archived prediction is compared with what was observed. The difference is the forecast error, and an error above the threshold is labelled a bust. Move the two sliders to try it.

                        Getting started

                        • Sign in with your user ID. Your role decides which workspace opens.
                        • Pick the forecast cycle in the blue bar. Every page then shows that cycle.
                        • On the dashboard, choose a lead day and select a subdivision on the map, or use the list below it.
                        • The card on the right explains the estimate: the main factors, the closest past case and the recommended action.
                        • After the day has verified, record what happened. A senior forecaster approves it before the model learns from it.
                        • Model Trust shows where the model itself is less sure.

                        Roles and permissions

                        Every person signs in with their own user ID and holds one role. Several people can hold the same role. Each role opens its own workspace: Shift desk, Review desk, Control room or Briefing.

                        Glossary

                        Forecast bust
                        A large deviation between a forecast and what actually happens.
                        Lead day
                        How many days ahead the forecast is for, from Day 1 to Day 10.
                        Subdivision
                        One of the 33 meteorological subdivisions Vishwas monitors.
                        Warning level
                        Green (no warning), Yellow (watch), Orange (alert) or Red (warning), following the IMD colour code.
                        Forecast confidence
                        100% minus the bust probability. How far the issued forecast can be trusted for a subdivision and lead day.
                        Weather event
                        The type of event most likely to cause a bust in a flagged subdivision, such as a cyclone or a heat wave.
                        Model self-confidence
                        How much historical precedent the model has for the current pattern. It is separate from bust risk.
                        Historical analog
                        A past event with a similar forecast pattern, shown with how its forecast verified.
                        Outcome
                        What a forecaster recorded after the fact: correct, incorrect or partial.
                        Approved outcome
                        An outcome that a senior forecaster has checked. Only approved outcomes are used to improve the model.

                        Accessibility

                        Use A-, A and A+ in the top bar to change the text size, and Dark mode for a darker theme that is easier on the eyes on night shifts. Every page works with a keyboard. Each subdivision on the map has a text label with its name, value and warning level, so colour is never the only signal.
