        About Vishwas

        What it does

        It looks at an already-issued NCMRWF and IMD forecast for Day 1 to Day 10 and estimates how likely that forecast is to bust, meaning a large deviation between the forecast and what actually happens, before the outcome is known. It flags which subdivisions and lead days deserve extra caution, so a duty forecaster can weigh the official guidance with calibrated confidence.

        What it does not do

        It does not forecast the weather itself, and it does not replace or override IMD or NCMRWF guidance. It does not issue public warnings, and it is not a substitute for the duty forecaster's judgment. It is a second opinion on how much to trust the first one.

        How the bust-risk estimate is built

        For each subdivision and lead day, the system compares characteristics of the current forecast pattern (moisture flux, upper-air trough position, sea-surface temperature anomalies, ensemble spread and related fields) against a historical record of past forecasts and their verified outcomes. Where the current pattern closely resembles situations that busted before, the estimate rises.

        Model self-confidence

        Reported separately, on a violet scale that never uses the warning colors. It reflects how much historical precedent exists for the current pattern, not how severe the weather is expected to be.

        Data sources

        ERA5 reanalysis

        ECMWF's global reanalysis, used as a verification reference for past atmospheric states.

        IMD Gridded Rainfall

        India Meteorological Department's gridded daily rainfall archive, used to verify past forecast outcomes at subdivision level.

        NCMRWF NWP archives

        Archived numerical weather prediction runs, used to build the historical bust record this tool learns from.
